# Purpose

- Own real 3D for Director: glTF binary scenes rendered by Bevy 0.20 on wgpu Vulkan and painted into Skia as one element.

## Ownership

- `src/accumulate.rs` - `Accumulator`: the Export level's `Rgba16Float` sample sum per target size and its resolve back into the target.
- `src/glb.rs` - `inspect`: the clip count, the longest clip duration and the camera count of a GLB, read from its JSON chunk with no GPU, `node_names`, the names of its nodes, and `empty`, the scene of a model made only of splats or type.
- `src/text.rs` - extruded type: `Text3d` (a font asset, one line of text, weight, stretch, size, depth, bevel, placement and PBR material) and `solid`, which builds its mesh on the CPU.
- `src/catcher.rs` and `src/catcher.wesl` - `CatcherPlugin` and the `ShadowCatcher` material of shadow catcher meshes, with its reflectance and mirror image.
- `src/display.rs` - `DisplayPlugin`: moves each view's sorted splat items out of the transparent phase and draws them over the tonemapped image against the main pass depth, before SMAA.
- `src/hero.rs` and `src/hero.wesl` - the Hero level: `HeroPlugin` and its compute pass, the denoiser's guide textures and passes, the `Lamp`s it samples, `traceable`, the Solari mesh copies (`solari_copy`), their rest pose and the CPU deform of skins and morph targets.
- `src/denoise.wesl` - one pass of the Hero denoiser's edge-aware à-trous filter.
- `src/lights.rs` - authored lights: spawning, the emitter sample points of soft shadows, the `Authored` marker the Hero pass reads, and the ray traced quads of rect lights.
- `src/lens.rs` - `overscan` and `Overscan`: the wide pinhole render behind a frame with radial distortion and the SkSL shader that bends it into the footage's lens as it paints.
- `src/gpu.rs` - the shared Vulkan device and `gpu_context`, which hands out Skia contexts on it.
- `src/node.rs` - `Asset` (bytes, content hash, loader extension, optional source path; `Asset::font` accepts TrueType, OpenType and collection files), `Splat` (a cloud asset and its placement), `ModelNode` (the `model` element), its document export, `decode`, `register_import` and `texture_draws`.
- `src/studio.rs` - the studio: one headless Bevy app on a `director-3d` thread, its stages, the orbit camera system, render targets, the shot lease, readback, texture shots, the equirectangular to cube conversion and the neutral studio probe.
- `tests/fixtures/make_model.py` - the Blender 5.2 script that writes `tests/fixtures/model.glb`: a turning cube, a rising sphere, a ground, a sun and a camera, exported as two 61-frame clips at 30 fps that place frame f at f / 30 seconds. With `-- --eevee crates/director-3d/tests/fixtures` it exports nothing and instead writes `sky.hdr` (a 128x64 equirectangular sky that depends only on elevation) and Eevee's 160x90 renders of frames 1 and 46 under that sky, `eevee-1.png` and `eevee-46.png`, over a transparent film with the Standard view transform. All five files are owned source; rerun the script after editing it.
- `tests/fixtures/make_track.py` - the Blender 5.2 script that writes the matchmove fixture from `sky.hdr`: `track.glb` (a ground stand-in named `Ground`, a blue box, a sun and a camera keyed on every frame as it dollies past the box over 61 frames at 30 fps), `track.mp4` (the plate: Eevee's 160x90 renders of frames 0 to 61 over a checkered ground with the box hidden, encoded H.264 yuv420p by ffmpeg, so plate frame k shows at k / 30 seconds like GLB frame k) and `track-1.png` and `track-46.png` (Eevee's renders of those frames with the box). - `tests/fixtures/make_solve.py` - the Blender 5.2 script that writes the camera solve fixture from `sky.hdr`: `solve.mp4` (Eevee's 480x270 renders of a handheld-height camera walking sideways past five textured crates and two pillars on a Voronoi yard over 60 frames at 30 fps while it pans, with the blue box hidden, H.264 yuv420p), `solve.json` (the true lens, frame 0's camera height and every frame's camera in the solve's world), `solve.glb` (the box, a `Ground` stand-in and the sun, with no camera, placed in that world) and `solve-10.png` and `solve-50.png` (Eevee's renders of those frames with the box). ## Local Contracts

- Compiling a model reads its bytes and `inspect`s them. The GPU starts on the first frame a model paints. `available()` starts the studio and reports whether a Vulkan adapter exists; tests that paint a model skip without one.
- The studio is one process-wide Bevy app with `WinitPlugin` disabled and `bevy_gaussian_splatting`'s plugin added. Each distinct (model hash, environment hash, splat set hash, shadow catcher hash, text set hash) loads once as a stage: its glTF scene root, every clip in one `AnimationGraph`, a Director camera, and a probe cube (the environment `.hdr` or `.exr` converted to a 512 px Rgba16Float cube, or a 64 px neutral studio probe). glTF cameras are deactivated and lights get shadow maps. The splat crate's scene loader also claims `.glb`, so a stage loads the `Gltf` by type, takes scene 0 and the clips from it and keeps the handle, because a dropped one unloads the file and the next stage's load would replace the shared scene and respawn every stage's instance.
- Each `Splat` spawns under the stage root as a `PlanarGaussian3d` cloud with its transform, `CloudSettings::global_opacity` and an `Aabb` of its visible gaussians (the splat draw queue needs one, and the crate's own spans the padding gaussians). The stage bounds take the 1st to 99th percentile of each cloud's gaussian centres, padded by three median scales. The crate sorts each cloud per view on the GPU with a radix sort, so the order follows each shot's camera. Its shader writes the captured colours display-referred, so `DisplayPlugin` draws the clouds after tonemapping: meshes take TonyMcMapface (and bloom with a background) on every stage, and the clouds keep their captured colours.
- `gpu.rs` opens one wgpu Vulkan device for the process with up to 8 queues in family 0. Bevy boots on it through `RenderCreation::Manual` with the features and limits Bevy requests itself, and renders on queue 0. Each `gpu_context()` call builds a Skia `DirectContext` on the next spare queue and returns `None` once they run out.
- A shot carries every input of its frame: stage, size, clip time, view, exposure, probe intensity and background. It hides other stages, seeks every clip and places the camera, so frames render the same in any order. A new stage or target size steps the app three extra times before its first frame so pipelines and the filtered probe exist. A warm stage that comes back into view, or whose light set changed, steps twice more, because its morph descriptors and its lights' shadow casters arrive late. Bevy copies the probe intensity into the generated `EnvironmentMapLight` once, so each shot writes it there too.
- Skinned meshes and morph targets play with the clips. Bevy drops a hidden stage's mesh instances and, when the stage shows again, collects a morph mesh before its morph descriptor exists, so the instance never morphs. A shot of a stage with morph targets marks those meshes changed, and switching to it from another stage steps the app once more first.
- `solid` shapes the line with Skia's HarfBuzz shaper (kerning and ligatures) at the `wght` and `wdth` positions of the weight and stretch, merges overlapping glyph contours with Skia's path ops and flattens each contour within 0.0015 em. Lyon triangulates the front and back faces from the contours moved inward by the bevel, and quads join them around the sides: a 45 degree chamfer at each face and a straight wall between. Normals stay smooth across curves and split at corners sharper than 35 degrees. The solid is closed with outward normals and centres on its advance width, its cap height above the baseline and its depth. Text with no visible glyphs fails. Each text spawns under the stage root as a parent placed by its position and rotation (about X, then Y, then Z) holding a faces mesh and a sides mesh, each with a `StandardMaterial` of its sRGB colour, metallic and perceptual roughness; the sides take `side_color` when it is set.
- A `ModelNode` with a `track` films each frame through the solved camera of the footage frame on show: frame floor(t * fps) of the element time, the first before it and the last after the footage ends. That `ShotCamera` (position, rotation and vertical field of view, near 0.05 m and far 2000 m) replaces the file's camera and the framing camera, `ModelView` leaves it alone, and the model varies with time while the frame changes. A track with radial distortion renders through `lens::overscan`: a pinhole camera at the same focal length in pixels, wide enough (up to three times the frame per axis) to hold every ray the distorted frame shows, whose image `Overscan::draw` paints through a shader that undistorts each output pixel's ray by fixed-point iteration and samples the wide render bilinearly. Import refuses a track without a positive fps, a field of view between 0 and 180 degrees, finite distortion and a frame.
- Without a track the camera starts from the file's first camera at this frame's animated pose and lens, or from a framing camera with a 30 degree vertical field of view that fits the bounding sphere. `ModelView` then orbits it about the bounding sphere centre: yaw about world Y, pitch about the camera's right axis (unclamped, so a full turn comes back to the start; positive raises the camera) and distance scaling the offset. The framing camera's near and far planes follow the orbit distance, so a close camera keeps the whole bounding sphere in front of its near plane.
- A perspective camera with an `aperture` (f-number on Bevy's Super 35 sensor, 18.66 mm tall) is a thin lens focused `focus` metres ahead, or at the bounding sphere centre's depth when `focus` is 0. The shot renders at least 16 samples at either level. Each sample moves the eye across the aperture disk (focal length / f-number wide, Halton (5, 7) through a concentric map) and shifts its sub view so the focus plane stays fixed in the image. Bevy's own depth of field pass writes opaque alpha and compiles a few frames late, so the studio does not use it.
- Frames render in HDR with TonyMcMapface, SMAA and screen-space transmission into an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` target that also allows an `Rgba8Unorm` view. Bevy renders through the sRGB view. A shot with `background` also takes `Bloom::NATURAL`. Without `background` the clear is transparent, the page shows through the sky, and the shot does not bloom, because the frame is a layer whose glow would haze the page or footage under it as added light.
- `shadow_catchers` names glTF nodes whose meshes show only the shadows cast on them. As a stage loads, every mesh at or below a named node swaps its `StandardMaterial` for the `ShadowCatcher` material, and a name that reaches no mesh fails the shot. `catcher.wesl` lights a white diffuse surface with every point, spot and directional light with and without its shadow map, plus the ambient and probe diffuse light, and writes black with alpha 1 - r^(1/2.2), where r is the shown fraction of that light. Pages and footage blend display-encoded, and a display curve near a 2.2 power turns a linear ratio r into r^(1/2.2), so a footage plate under a transparent model darkens as far as the real ground would. The material blends premultiplied with no prepass and casts no shadow, so it draws in the transparent pass at every level, over the Hero image too, and Solari never traces it. Catcher meshes sit on render layer 1, which the Director camera sees beside layer 0. A shot with `reflection` above 0 also mirrors the model in its catchers, which must lie in one plane (fit from their triangles at stage load; a curved or folded set fails the shot). A mirror camera per stage, the Director camera reflected in that plane and flipped along its own X so it stays a rotation, renders layer 0 before the Director camera into an `Rgba16Float` image of the shot size: linear radiance at the same exposure and probe, no tone curve, dither, bloom or splats, a transparent clear, and the probe sky only with `background`. Its sample offset mirrors the Director camera's, so jitter and the lens match. The catcher samples that image at (1 - u, v), takes Schlick's Fresnel F with F0 the reflection and k = F times the mirrored coverage a, and writes the mirrored colour times k^2.2 / a at alpha 1 - (1 - shadow)(1 - k). The k^2.2 undoes the display encoding of a premultiplied blend, as the shadow's r^(1/2.2) does, so a strength scales the reflected light linearly. The mirror camera rasterizes at every level, so a Hero shot reflects the raster image. Geometry below the plane is not clipped from the mirror. A matchmove puts the plate under the model and the file's solved camera drives the shot. Each target remembers the shot it holds and refilms only when a shot input changes.
- A pixel shot reads the target back as premultiplied sRGB RGBA8. A texture shot moves it to `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL` and returns the raw `VkImage`. Every shot holds the studio lease, and a texture shot's `Lease` keeps it until Skia has sampled the image, so no shot refilms a target in between.
- A shot's `ModelLight`s spawn under its stage root beside the file's lights; `lights.rs` resolves their default position and target against the bounding sphere. A sun is a `DirectionalLight` whose first cascade ends at 4 scene radii or 5 m, whichever is nearer, and whose last reaches 16 radii or 1000 m, whichever is farther, so a wide ground under a small subject keeps sharp shadows, point and spot lights are Bevy's, and a rect light is a spot with an 85 degree cone and no inner cone whose squared falloff stands in for a softbox's cosine, scaled so it emits the given lumens. A light with an emitter size (a sun's `angle`, a point or spot `radius`, every rect) raises the shot to 16 samples, and each sample moves it to a Halton (11, 13) point on its disc or rectangle, or tilts a sun within its angle, with hard shadows from there. The stage respawns the lights when the set changes.
- `samples` above 1 is the Export level, and a lens or a sized light raises a shot to 16 samples. The studio renders the shot that many times with a sub-pixel `SubCameraView` offset from the Halton (2, 3) sequence, which enters the projection itself so meshes and splats (whose shader reads the unjittered projection) move together; `orbit` runs before `CameraUpdateSystems` so each sample's projection is its own, adds 1/N of each render's linear colour to the sum and resolves the sum through the target's sRGB view. Only the last sample waits for the GPU. One sample renders without jitter. Preview and export render the same sample count, so they match pixel for pixel at either level.
- `paths` above 0 (up to `MAX_PATHS`, 4096) is the Hero level, path traced through `bevy_solari`'s ray traced scene; `traceable()` reports whether the device has its ray query features. On a stage's first Hero shot each mesh gets a copy in Solari's layout (positions, normals, UVs and tangents with U32 indices) as its `RaytracingMesh3d`, and only the shot's stage stays in the acceleration structure. Each sample dispatches `hero.wesl` after the opaque and transmissive passes and before the transparent pass: `paths` paths per pixel with random pixel jitter, emissive, sun and BRDF sampling weighted by multiple importance, Russian roulette and up to 32 bounces, written as premultiplied radiance times exposure with alpha as coverage. A camera ray that misses shows the probe with `background` and a transparent sky without it. Tonemapping, bloom, SMAA and the sample average follow as at the other levels. Seeds come from the pixel, sample and path, so a frame depends only on its inputs. A shot with `denoise` traces through `hero_guided` instead, which writes exposed radiance over the mean first-hit albedo (the base colour, at least 0.02) with the variance of its luminance, and the mean first-hit position, normal and albedo, into textures from Bevy's `TextureCache`. Five à-trous passes of `denoise.wesl` then blur the radiance with a 5x5 B3 kernel whose taps sit 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 pixels apart, weighted by the normals' dot product to the 128th power, the distance off the centre's tangent plane in pixel footprints, the hit fractions and the luminance difference against 4 standard deviations of the 3x3-blurred variance, carrying the variance with squared weights. The last pass multiplies the albedo back and writes the view target with the pixel's own coverage; a pixel whose camera rays all missed keeps its value. The filter reads only the sample's own guides, so a denoised frame still depends only on its inputs. Hero samples keep the pixel grid and still move the lens and sized suns. Solari has no point or spot lights, so a `PostUpdate` system gives the Hero camera every visible point and spot light (the file's and the authored ones) as a `Lamp` in Bevy's raster units, and each non-mirror hit samples them all with a shadow ray: inverse square falloff, Bevy's range window and spot cone, and a disc of the light's radius facing the hit for soft shadows. Rect lights trace as one-sided quads facing the target that emit their lumens and respawn with the light set; the pass skips their stand-in spots. Skins and morph targets deform on the CPU into the copies before the samples. A `Traced` dispatch counter shared with the render world holds the first frame until a trace ran and fails a shot whose samples did not each trace once. The transparent pass then draws splat clouds over the traced image against the raster depth, and blended meshes hide from the raster for the shot because Solari traces them as opaque. A model made only of splats has nothing to trace and rasterizes. Solari binds no scene without a sun or an emissive mesh, so a Hero stage lit only by the probe and its lamps gets a sun of 0 lux without shadow maps. A Hero shot fails on a GPU without ray tracing.
- `ModelNode` on a canvas whose context came from `gpu_context` borrows the texture as an `R8G8B8A8_UNORM` sRGB Skia image, draws it, syncs the context and counts the draw in `texture_draws()`. Any other canvas paints the pixel shot at the device size of its layout box and caches the last frame by every shot input. Clip time is the element time, wrapped at the longest clip with `looped` and held at its ends otherwise.
- A document records the model as a `NodeBody::Model`: a `model/gltf-binary` asset (by path when the source has one, embedded otherwise), an optional `image/vnd.radiance` or `image/x-exr` environment, exposure, probe intensity, background, loop, the view before animation and each splat as an `application/x-ply` asset with its placement. Each text records its font as a `font/ttf`, `font/otf` or `font/collection` asset (by path when the source has one) beside its string and the `Text3d` numbers. A model made only of splats or type exports its empty scene as an embedded GLB. `decode` rebuilds the same element, with `samples` clamped to 1 to 256 and `paths` to at most 4096.
- `director-3d` never links a second Skia: it depends on the workspace `skia-safe`, and Bevy's `wgpu` stays at the version Bevy pins.
